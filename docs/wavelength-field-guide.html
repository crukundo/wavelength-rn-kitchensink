00

The two questions that decide it

Kesh shelved its last Ark attempt (bark) for two specific failures: an inactive wallet that silently lost money while still showing it as spendable, and a maintenance step that blocked receiving. Everything else is secondary to how Wavelength answers these two.

L1 · phantom balance

Does an idle wallet still lose money?

Answered by design, while running

A real per-VTXO expiry state machine drives refresh and unilateral exit, and the spendable figure counts only live VTXOs. Not yet tested through a real closure — a reopened wallet can briefly show value it no longer holds until it processes a block.

L2 · receive block

Can maintenance still starve receiving?

Root cause known · fix not in v0.1.0

One receive in five runs hung for ten minutes and failed. The wait is a ten-minute constant inside the on-device daemon, waiting on a reply from the operator. Lightning Labs fixed the same cause after v0.1.0 was cut — the release we run does not carry it.

01

How value lives in Wavelength

Ark holds your money as virtual UTXOs — VTXOs — kept off-chain and backed by an on-chain output the operator controls. A VTXO is not a coin you hold outright. It is a lease with an expiry height, and keeping it alive is active work.

Every VTXO carries a batch expiry. As that height approaches, the client moves it through four states and acts on each. The thresholds are dynamic, derived from the VTXO's own tree depth and timelock so there is always time to exit to chain rather than lose the funds.

safe
plenty of time
no action; balance counts it as spendable
needs_refresh
≤ 144 blocks*
request a cooperative forfeit into a fresh round, resetting the lease
critical
≤ max(36, depth×6 + CSV)
escalate to the chain resolver for unilateral exit rather than wait
expired
past batch height
marked failed, not recoverable; the operator reclaims the backing

* refresh threshold = max(144, critical + 72), and never traded away to chase the operator's free-refresh window. Source: vtxo/expiry.go:81–90, transitions.go:236.

Source

The lease is real, and defended in code

Four states, dynamic thresholds, and a documented rule to never trade safety for a fee waiver. An expired VTXO moves to a failed, non-recoverable state.

vtxo/expiry.go · vtxo/transitions.go:176,236

Unverified

A closed app defends nothing

The daemon runs in-process, so a force-quit stops all refresh and escalation. That a closed wallet eventually loses funds is a strong inference, not source-proven, and no background execution was found in this harness.

inference from in-process daemon

Observed

Rounds are the operator's clock

Value settles in batched operator rounds. Measured round times ranged from 94 to 1,655 seconds with no pattern — about 2 to 28 minutes. A wallet does not control when its round runs.

5 probe runs, signet, 24 & 28 July 2026

A client cannot ask for a round

There is no refresh RPC. Only two client actions do round work: a cooperative exit, and boarding a deposit. Automatic refresh near expiry cannot be triggered on demand.

02

The stack on a native client

Wavelength is not a thin client talking to a server. The full daemon is a native library compiled into the app, so the phone runs its own Ark node. Your JavaScript calls a native binding, which drives that in-process daemon, which talks to three remote services.

The mailbox transport matters for one reason. A unary call registers a response waiter, sends the request, then blocks on that waiter. If the reply never comes back, the call waits out a fixed ten-minute time-to-live — which is the whole of section 05.

03

What you must know to embed it

These are the constraints that cost real payments to discover. Each carries its provenance and the design decision it forces. The recurring theme: almost nothing is a fixed constant, and the client hides most of what it depends on.

Source

Almost every limit is operator policy, and the SDK hides it

The operator negotiates 11 policy values — dust limit, VTXO floor, balance cap, fee rate, required confirmations and more. The SDK's ServerInfo exposes exactly one: freeRefreshWindowBlocks. A client cannot read the VTXO floor, so it cannot compute the true maximum sendable.

lib/types/boarding.go:34 · generated.d.ts:273

Design implicationNever hardcode a threshold. Read the terms — or have a backend supply them (section 06) — and render limits from them. A number seen on signet is one operator's policy, not the protocol.
Observed

Spendable ≠ balance

A wallet holding one 1,745 sat VTXO could not send 1,000: change of 745 fell below the operator's minimum change of 1,000. Selection rejects sub-minimum change unless change is exactly zero.

rpc_server.go:3104 · coinselect/selector.go:53

Design implicationCompute the real maximum from the VTXO inventory and reject at amount entry. Exact-VTXO "send max" always works — offer it as the escape hatch.
Observed

The balance moves on its own

Refresh fees, round-join fees, change re-minting and credit auto-redeem all change the spendable figure with no user action. A boarding deposit of 2,000 credited 1,745 after a 255 fee.

waved/rpc_fees.go:49 · credit/policy.go:17

Design implicationNever present a balance delta as the user's payment. "You sent 1,000" and "your balance fell by 1,255" are both true and must be shown as separate, named facts.
Observed

The rail is an output of the quote

A destination does not fix how a payment travels. A 500 sat BOLT11 send quoted on the credit rail on a wallet holding only Ark VTXOs. Rails: in-ark, lightning, onchain, credit, mixed.

destination.d.ts:5 · generated.d.ts:385

Design implicationNo screen before the quote may promise a rail or a fee. Show them only once the quote returns.
Observed

Small VTXOs are near-unexitable

A 1,000 sat VTXO exited cooperatively arrived on-chain as 743 — a quarter lost to fees. A below-floor 744 sat VTXO could still be exited, since the minimum limits what a send may produce, not what an exit may forfeit.

observed 24 & 28 July 2026

Design implicationTell the user the per-VTXO exit cost before they board into a shape made of many small pieces.
Source

Deposits auto-join the next round

A confirmed boarding deposit joins the next round with no user action — the wallet checks the tip every second and joins eagerly. Total time is the operator's required confirmations plus one full round.

wallet/wallet.go:54,208 · round/actor.go:1343

Design implication"Pending" after a deposit is expected and can last many minutes. Confirmations required is operator policy and is not exposed to the client.
Source

Send intents are single-use, by design

A prepared send expires in 5 minutes and any dispatch failure burns it — the caller must prepare again. A failed Lightning send surfaced AlreadyExists: receive intent already used, which appears to kill the payee's invoice too.

swapwallet/send_intents.go:15,92

Design implicationAfter a failed send, never offer a retry that reuses the quote or the invoice. Prepare again; the payee likely needs a fresh invoice.
Observed

Deposits and exits emit no live event

The activity stream carries swap and send entries only. A confirmed boarding deposit did not appear until a manual refresh. Screen-scoped polls cap at ~10 minutes — about one signet block — so a poll can expire before its confirmation.

usePollWhileWaiting.ts:34,42 · wavelength#875

Design implicationDo not rely on the stream for money-in. Reconcile on foreground and on a schedule, and always expose a manual refresh — even on an empty wallet.
Source

Some funds cannot be unilaterally exited

Two exit-infeasibility reasons are structural and permanent: sweep_below_dust and uneconomical. Unilateral exit also needs fee UTXOs in the backing wallet; forced unroll requires a literal I_KNOW_WHAT_I_AM_DOING.

generated.d.ts:849,873

Design implicationA wallet can hold value it cannot escape without the operator. That is a hole in the self-custody claim — surface it at deposit time, not exit time.
Unverified

Credit totals can read as zero

Credit numbers are quoted by the closed swap server, not computed locally. A 500 sat send reported a required 1,000 top-up, and a credit-rail quote can report total outflow of zero while the top-up is a real cost.

swapwallet/router.go:777,790

Design implicationDefine "total" as total cost to the user, including any credit top-up. A payment that shows a total of 0 is not comprehensible.
04

The balance model

Balance is one atomic snapshot of five independent fields, not a running total. It is the only source of truth for value — deriving it from activity, or activity from it, produced double-counted totals. Only one field is spendable.

confirmed_sat
spendable. Sum of live VTXOs only, traced end to end from SumSpendableBalance(ListLiveVTXOs)
pending_in_sat
boarding only — confirmed, unconfirmed and adopted deposits. An unpaid invoice leaves this at zero
pending_out_sat
pending boarding sweep, and a queued cooperative exit (contrary to the code comment)
credit_available_sat
server-side credit ledger, spendable via the credit rail
credit_reserved_sat
credit committed to an in-flight operation

Two facts sit behind that green field. Spendable is live-VTXO only by deliberate design — mapping the conflated total onto confirmed_sat "would tell the user they have spendable balance immediately after a faucet deposit, before any round commit". And an expired VTXO only leaves the live set once the client processes a block epoch, so a wallet reopened after a long closure can briefly overstate what it holds. That transient window is exactly bark's symptom, and is what test L1 must measure.

The snapshot also excludes the on-chain backing wallet entirely; this harness reads that figure separately by previewing a sweep. Source: swapwallet/service.go:595, waved/rpc_server.go:750, src/lib/balance.ts:5.

05

The ten-minute receive block

This is the headline risk, and it is no longer a mystery. On one run of five, creating an invoice hung for 602,812 milliseconds and failed with an internal error — the same user-visible failure that shelved bark. Reading the source settled where the wait lives and why it lasts ten minutes.

The failing call registers a receive script with the operator's indexer over the mailbox. The daemon's AwaitRPC blocked on a reply from the operator that never arrived, and waited out a fixed time-to-live. That TTL is a constant: DefaultResponseWaiterTTL = 10 * time.Minute, wired everywhere as the default and exposed nowhere. The error surfaces up seven frames, all in the public source:

start receiveswapwallet/recv.go:96
start receive swapswapclientserver/service.go:1481
allocate claim receive scriptsdk/swaps/out_swap.go:652
create receive scriptsdk/ark/client.go:629
unable to create OOR receive scriptwaved/rpc_oor_receive.go:74
register receive script → response waiter expiredreceive_script.go:477 · response_registry.go:16
Why the release we run is exposed

Lightning Labs diagnosed this exact cause on a different RPC in issue #1041 and fixed it in PR #1044 — client-side gRPC keepalive so a silently dead operator connection is caught in seconds, not ten minutes. It merged on 23 July 2026. v0.1.0 was tagged on 21 July and carries no backport, so our build dials the operator with no keepalive.

Keepalive shortens the dead-connection case; it does not bound the waiter. The mailbox and connection code is byte-identical between v0.1.0 and main, so a reply lost for any other reason still hangs for the full ten minutes. We measured the server-side half already live on signet: six streamless pings, 30 seconds apart, all acknowledged.

Round execution and round duration are both ruled out as the trigger: four runs executed rounds, two of them longer than the blocked run, all clean. The round now looks like a coincidence.

The client cannot fully defend itself against this today. The SDK's receive() takes no signal or timeout, so a wallet can race the promise and stop waiting, but cannot cancel the call — the abandoned call runs on to the TTL inside the daemon. That is harmless only if a stuck call holds nothing else, which is the one thing left to confirm with a forced reproduction.

06

Where a backend earns its place

Wavelength is self-custodial and runs entirely on the device; there is no server SDK to call. A backend's value is therefore specific — supply what the client hides, watch what a closed app cannot, and reconcile what the stream drops. Each recommendation below names the verified fact it rests on.

A

Serve the operator terms the SDK withholds

Run a daemon or terms oracle backend-side, read the full policy set, and hand the app the VTXO floor, balance cap and fee inputs. Then the app can compute the true maximum sendable and reject impossible amounts at entry instead of at a failed confirmation.

Rests on ServerInfo exposes 1 of 11 policy values (generated.d.ts:273)

B

Own the L1 refresh the device cannot guarantee

A closed app runs no refresh, and iOS background execution cannot be relied on. A backend agent — a delegated or watchtower-style refresher — is the only way to keep an idle user's VTXOs alive. Absent that, cap how long value may sit in Ark and tell the user plainly.

Rests on in-process daemon; no background capability found (inference)

C

Surface the expiry deadline, don't wait for the loss

Every VTXO exposes its batch expiry and the current block height, so blocks-remaining is computable today with no SDK change. A backend can watch this and notify — "open the app before block N to keep this balance safe" — turning a silent loss into a visible deadline.

Rests on WalletVTXO.batchExpiry + Info.blockHeight (generated.d.ts:573,263)

D

Reconcile from the balance snapshot, never from activity

Do not build a running-balance ledger from Ark activity — the codebase forbids it, and it double-counts. Mirror the rule server-side: treat the balance snapshot as truth, use activity only as history, and drive money-in detection from reconciliation, not the live stream.

Rests on balance is the sole source of truth (src/lib/balance.ts:5); deposits emit no event (#875)

E

Wrap every call in a deadline; treat receive as retry-safe

Race each call against a short client deadline so a lost reply fails in seconds, not ten minutes. Retrying a receive is safe — an unpaid invoice costs nothing and expires. But a send is not retry-safe against the same intent, so classify outcomes by the typed failure code, never by error strings.

Rests on receive() has no timeout; 10-min TTL; EntryFailureCode enum (generated.d.ts:1172)

F

Coordinate invoice and intent lifecycles

A failed payer attempt appears to burn the payee's receive intent, killing that invoice. If a backend issues invoices for users, it must treat each as single-use, detect the burn, and mint a fresh invoice rather than surfacing a reused-intent error to either side.

Rests on intent burn is deliberate (send_intents.go:92); payee-side burn unverified, swap server closed

Still open
  • L1 through a real closure. Measure how long after reopen the balance corrects, and confirm the operator reclaims the backing. Both are inferred, not observed.
  • Whether a stuck receive holds the wallet. One forced-blackhole reproduction distinguishes a single lost reply from a wallet-wide stall — the difference between a retryable glitch and bark's failure.
  • What this operator actually advertises. Every number here is one operator's policy on signet. None can be read from the client.
  • The payee-side intent burn. Needs the closed swap-server source or a controlled regtest reproduction.
